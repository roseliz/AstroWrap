Finding test grounds on Earth for the Moon and Mars

Before sending a rover, a lander or astronauts, we test them on Earth, in places that look like the target site. This tool looks for those places: you paint an area on the Moon or on Mars, and it ranks the whole Earth by how much it resembles that area: terrain, temperatures, wind, ice, rocks.

How it works

  1. Choose a body and paint an area On the globe of the Moon or Mars, you paint the target area with hexagonal tiles (H3): a crater, a plain, a pole.
  2. The area becomes a reference For each criterion, the server averages the data over the area. The data are prepared in advance for the Moon, Mars and Earth on the same grid (H3 cells at resolution 5, ~250 km² on Earth).
  3. The whole Earth is compared The 613,252 land cells are scored criterion by criterion, then each candidate is judged over an area as large as the painted one. Anyone can adjust how much each criterion counts; places at sea or covered by forests and crops are set aside.
  4. Read and check For each site: why it looks alike, criterion by criterion, the terrain side by side, and your AstroWrap, an animated recap card by card, down to what testing there would cost the Earth. Below the list, the analogs already known to scientists are shown at their real rank.

What the tool compares

  • Terrain slopes, flat ground, elevation range, crater density
  • Temperature ground by day and by night, day-night range, sunshine
  • Rocks share of each rock type (basalt, sediments, ice…)
  • Wind and dust strong winds, bare ground, dunes, dust storms (Mars)
  • Ice and water ice in the ground, surface ice, soil water
  • Volcanoes and caves volcanoes, caves, lava tubes

What it does not compare

  • Minerals. The maps of Mars and the Moon only give pyroxene and olivine, which the Earth instruments do not see. Mineralogy analogs (Rio Tinto for Meridiani, Lake Salda for Jezero) are therefore out of reach.
  • Small features. A site is judged over hundreds of km²: a lake, a spring or an isolated cave gets lost in it.
  • What no place on Earth reproduces: gravity, air pressure, radiation.

Responsible testing

The best analog is not always the right place to test: some are fragile, protected or far away. Below the list of sites, the tool says what a test campaign would cost the Earth.

  • Protected area: at least half of the site in protected areas, or a fifth in strict protection (IUCN categories Ia, Ib and II: strict reserves, wilderness areas, national parks). An option sets these places aside.
  • Low impact: less than 10% protected, on land already transformed by mining (at least 0.5% of the surface). Testing in a former mine rather than in an untouched desert.
  • The flight: from your departure city, the round trip by plane in tonnes of CO₂e per person, and the best site within 1,000 to 5,000 km that is at least 85% as similar as the first one.

The World Database on Protected Areas (UNEP-WCMC and IUCN, Protected Planet) is used for non-commercial research: the tool only shows a share of protected land per cell, and the database is not redistributed. The version used is cited below the list of sites.

The Mars Climate Database (Laboratoire de Météorologie Dynamique, with CNES and ESA; Forget et al. 1999, Journal of Geophysical Research 104, E10) is used with the agreement of its team: the tool only shows values derived per cell, and the database itself is not redistributed.
